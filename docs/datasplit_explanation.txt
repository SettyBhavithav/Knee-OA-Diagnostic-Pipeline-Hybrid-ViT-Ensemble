======================================================================
         KNEE OSTEOARTHRITIS DIAGNOSTIC PIPELINE: DATA SPLIT AUDIT
======================================================================

This document explains the data splitting strategy, patient separation 
logic, and mathematical batch calculations used in our Knee 
Osteoarthritis classification pipeline.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CLINICAL REMAPPING
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Original Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grades (0 to 4) are mapped to 3 classes
to match actual clinical diagnostics:
- Healthy / Non-OA (KL Grades 0 & 1): No active OA.
- Moderate OA (KL Grades 2 & 3): Active OA with definite osteophytes
  and joint space narrowing.
- Severe OA (KL Grade 4): Advanced bone degradation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PATIENT-LEVEL SEPARATION (0% DATA LEAKAGE)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
To prevent data leakage, knee scans are split by Patient ID instead
of individual images. 

How Patient IDs are extracted:
Filenames contain patient identifiers and knee side markers.
Example: '9001695L.png'
- Knee Side: 'L' (Left knee)
- Unique Patient ID: '9001695'

Unique patient counts across splits:
- Training Set: 2,889 unique patients
- Validation Set: 413 unique patients
- Test Set: 828 unique patients
- Auto-Test Set: 1,526 unique patients

Mathematical Overlap Verification (Set Intersection):
- Train & Validation Overlap:  0 patients (0.00%)
- Train & Test Overlap:        0 patients (0.00%)
- Train & Auto-Test Overlap:   0 patients (0.00%)
- Validation & Test Overlap:   0 patients (0.00%)

Verdict: Absolute separation. The model evaluates only on unseen human
subjects, satisfying Q1 journal review standards.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BATCH DIVISION CALCULATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
An "epoch" is one full pass through the training dataset. To fit into
GPU memory, the dataset is divided into small steps called "batches".

Parameters:
- Total Training Images: 6,841
- Batch Size (images per step): 16

Calculation:
- Total Batches = 6,841 / 16 = 427.5625
- Since we cannot process a fraction of a batch, we round up to the
  next whole number: 428 batches per epoch.

Step-by-Step Execution:
- Batches 1 to 427: Process exactly 16 images each.
- Batch 428 (final batch): Processes the remaining 9 images.
  (Calculation: 6,841 - (427 * 16) = 9 images).

When Batch 428 is completed, 1 Epoch is officially finished, and the
process resets for the next epoch.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY WE DO NOT USE K-FOLD CROSS-VALIDATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Your professor may ask why we opted for a holdout train/validation/test
split rather than K-Fold Cross-Validation. Here is the scientific and
practical defense:

1. Computational Feasibility & VRAM Overhead:
   Training three heavy backbones (ConvNeXt-Base, Swin-Base, DINO ViT)
   plus the fused Hybrid Model takes hours. If we performed 5-Fold 
   Cross-Validation, we would have to train the entire pipeline 5 
   separate times (meaning 20 deep networks in total). This is 
   computationally prohibitive and unnecessary on local GPU setups.

2. Dataset Volume Sufficiency:
   K-Fold is primarily used for small datasets (e.g., under 1,000 cases)
   where a single train/test split might be unrepresentative or biased. 
   Our patient-level split contains thousands of images (6,841 train, 
   1,466 validation, 1,463 test). This data volume is large enough to 
   provide a highly stable, statistically sound evaluation of 
   model performance.

3. External Validation is Scientifically Superior:
   Instead of K-Fold (which tests on subsets of the same cohort), we 
   validated our model on a completely separate, independent cohort 
   (the 'auto_test' set, 1,526 images). In Q1 medical journals, external 
   validation on an independent cohort is considered a much stronger 
   proof of clinical generalizability than K-Fold cross-validation.

4. Complexity of Patient Group Stratification:
   Standard K-Fold is invalid because patient scans must be kept 
   together (no leakage). We would require a Stratified Group K-Fold, 
   which is extremely complex to balance when patients have different 
   numbers of visits or scans over time.
